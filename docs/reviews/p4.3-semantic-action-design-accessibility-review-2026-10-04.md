## Initial substantive findings

### S3-AI01 — Medium — Radio requires a nonexistent Selection container

The initial S3 table at lines 77-78 and native matrix at lines 256-258 require
Selection observation and current container selection for radio selection as
well as List/Tab/ComboBox selection. That does not match the accepted fixture
and collector mapping:

- `FixtureSubjects.cs:28-29` defines radio-a/radio-b as SelectionItem subjects
  under fixture-window, which is not a Selection-family container.
- `Fixture/Program.cs:67-68,108-110` uses two named-group RadioButtons directly
  in the main content structure. It does not introduce a SelectionPattern group.
- `ReadOnlyCollector.cs:172-176,298-305` deliberately excludes RadioButton from
  SelectionContainer reads while preserving explicit selected state and current
  native/ancestry proof. The golden radio case verifies both selected bits and
  fixture `radio`, not a fabricated Selection container.

Microsoft defines
[SelectionContainer](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.windows.automation.selectionitempattern.selectionitempatterninformation.selectioncontainer?view=windowsdesktop-10.0)
as an element supporting SelectionPattern and documents a null default. Its
[RadioButton contract](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/winauto/uiauto-supportradiobuttoncontroltype)
also distinguishes SelectionItem from Toggle and discusses grouping limitations.
Neither the graph parent nor a null/default return proves a Selection container.

Required design correction: separate a closed fixture-radio profile from
List/Tab/ComboBox container selection. Prove the exact current radio pair and
their admitted ancestry/group membership, explicit reciprocal selected bits
before and after, and the fixture radio effect. Reject missing, duplicated,
unknown or replaced peers. Do not invent SelectionPattern on the Window or
interpret a default/null container as authority. Keep explicit current
SelectionContainer equality and Selection reciprocity for List/Tab/ComboBox.
This is a static feasibility defect, not a measured runtime observation.

### S3-AI02 — Medium — Combo expansion is not represented by the fixture expanded field

Initial line 79 requires desired expansion state and descendant/auxiliary
membership to agree with authoritative fixture state for both Group and the
proposed ComboBox profile. The unchanged fixture cannot provide the same
expansion oracle for both:

- `Fixture/Program.cs:199` records `combo` as the selected option only.
- `Fixture/Program.cs:201` records `expanded = expand.IsExpanded`, the separate
  Group/Expander. It does not record ComboBox IsDropDownOpen.
- The existing golden ComboBox snapshot has its own raw-observed
  `state.expanded`, while fixture `expanded` remains the Group's unrelated bit.

Required design correction: define the exact evidence split. Group expansion
checks fresh Group state/descendants and fixture `expanded`. Combo expansion
and collapse check fresh explicit Combo ExpandCollapse state, strict current
OS-admitted popup identity/membership or retirement, and unchanged fixture
combo selection plus unrelated fields. Do not use the Group bit as a Combo
oracle, alter the accepted fixture to add a field, or copy oracle values into
an observation. The design must state what its unchanged oracle can and cannot
prove before requiring positive dual evidence.

### S3-AI03 — Medium — Range step and accepted numeric subset are undefined

Initial line 80 requires a finite current min/max/step and inputs satisfying
that step, but advertises finite JSON numbers 0..10. The accepted range
descriptor and S2 observation contain value/minimum/maximum/read-only, not a
step or a provider-supported value lattice (`SemanticProjection.cs:148-149`;
`ReadOnlyCollector.cs:166-168`; P4.2 normalizer lines 120-121).

`Fixture/Program.cs:71` specifies TickFrequency=1 and IsSnapToTickEnabled, but
these WPF thumb/tick settings are not an explicit UIA legal-SetValue grid.
Microsoft's
[SmallChange](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.windows.automation.rangevaluepattern.rangevaluepatterninformation.smallchange?view=windowsdesktop-10.0)
describes an increment/decrement amount and documents a default; it does not
establish a required SetValue lattice. Microsoft separately documents
[TickFrequency](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.windows.controls.slider.tickfrequency?view=windowsdesktop-10.0)
and [snap-to-tick behavior](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.windows.controls.slider.issnaptotickenabled?view=windowsdesktop-10.0).
[RangeValue.SetValue](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.windows.automation.rangevaluepattern.setvalue?view=windowsdesktop-10.0)
allows provider-dependent rounding, making exact post-read verification essential.

Required design correction: choose and publish the exact closed numeric policy
now. A deliberately restricted integer 0..10 profile is possible if identified
as host policy, separately represented from the unchanged number-valued golden
descriptor. Alternatively retain finite numbers with explicit actual min/max
admission and exact requested-versus-observed verification. Identify any added
property requirement and its unknown/default semantics. Never infer a legal
grid from SmallChange/default zero, silently clamp or accept a rounded mismatch.
Post-call mismatches remain possible-effect/verification uncertainty, not proof
that no mutation occurred. This finding does not claim actual fixture rounding.

## Other reviewed constraints and observations

The initial design correctly separates seven pattern families from six mutation
names plus collapse: Selection is observation, not a seventh independent
mutation. The unchanged manifest has Combo actions none; a new closed Combo
profile and state-dependent Group collapse must remain distinct from the
unaltered descriptive comparison. Duplicate labels cannot choose a target.

Toggle is correctly one transition on the reviewed two-state fixture CheckBox
(`IsThreeState=false`), with explicit On/Off and no Indeterminate-to-false
conversion. Radio selection must remain SelectionItem rather than Toggle.
The proposed Value UTF-16/well-formedness cap is an additional host restriction,
not permission to alter the unchanged golden string schema or interpolate text
into commands/policy. Absence of input is distinct from a supplied null payload.

Two further feasibility clarifications should be retained during refinement:

- Invoke can block, not only Expand. The unchanged modal handler calls
  `ShowDialog` (`Fixture/Program.cs:169-176`).
  [Microsoft Invoke](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.windows.automation.invokepattern.invoke?view=windowsdesktop-10.0)
  makes immediate return provider-dependent and identifies modal blocking.
  Under the proposed single worker/no-parallel-provider rule, a blocked call
  cannot be converted into positive proof by silently adding a helper or a
  second mutation to unblock it. The accepted hard supervisor boundary must
  preserve possible-effect uncertainty. This is a documented risk/static handler
  observation, not proof that this fixture's UIA call blocks on this host.
- Combo Select may retire popup item references. Postconditions must use newly
  proved current selected relationships, not retained peer calls. If same-surface
  effect proof is possible while the original target is absent, the existing
  `targetPresent:false` response must remain honest and generation-bound; a new
  item reference cannot revive the retired one. Otherwise refuse success and
  retain uncertainty. No automatic expand/collapse may be hidden inside select.

Already-satisfied intents need an unambiguous catalog rule: either the current
state exposes only the opposite transition and the absent action is unavailable,
or an explicitly bound no-op intent is admitted with zero calls. A stale action
descriptor must not become an implicit no-op success after a revision change.
The initial freshness/revision rules are conservative; this is clarification,
not an identified safety relaxation in source.

The proposed ordering after every policy/confirmation/precondition await,
single-call mark-before-invoke, independent postcapture/oracle comparison,
single-use consent and bounded replay tombstones are appropriate design
requirements. Detected reset/window/process replacement must invalidate old
authority even if fixture state is identical. Generation loss cannot be disguised
as target absence or successful execution on a replacement surface. Core failure
versus native request-error separation and unchanged session third-revision
checks are preserved; provider throw/cancellation never proves no effect.

Shared 10-second cooperative accounting and the unchanged owned-worker hard
deadline bound the attempt without guaranteeing provider getter allocation or
per-call latency. The later matrix still needs frozen exact IDs, setup, effect
and unrelated-field expectations, zero/one-call instrumentation, original bytes,
independent verifier, and real first/repeat artifact reviews before execution.
Pure tests, documentation and the existing 22 golden summaries cannot supply
those actual native action proofs.

### Final authority, sequencing and feasibility disposition

I independently reviewed the additional reservation-order wording, rather than
carrying the intermediate target's disposition forward. Final step 1 reserves
the bounded supervisor-owned sole attempt slot after pure admission and before
launching/dispatching a mutation-capable worker. Native proof and policy remain
under that same reservation in steps 2/3; step 4 verifies its still-current
binding and consumes confirmation without allocating another slot. This removes
the previous text's ordering ambiguity without moving native authority into
the supervisor or loosening the immediate pre-call fence.

The final design retains explicit safety, reviewed types/patterns, current
ancestry/container qualification and fresh complete proof after every wait;
no first-match/default/cached peer grants eligibility. One mutation-may-start
mark precedes the sole pattern call. Postconditions require fresh UIA and
separately admitted fixture evidence with exact unrelated-field exclusions.
Generation loss cannot be relabeled as target absence or successful execution
on a replacement surface. Toggle stays one reviewed two-state transition;
Selection stays observation. Unknown semantic/safety/read-only states deny
execution rather than defaulting safe. Value/input restrictions and descriptive
versus executable catalogs remain separate and source-bound prerequisites.

The revised replay requirements also avoid promising historical disclosure at
a newer revision. They require terminal-revision equality and fresh current
identity/permission/policy, reject changed content/new-request stale-key cases,
distinguish verifier closure from an admitted wire error, and invalidate old
authority before disclosing an inadmissible historical result. Supervisor-owned
possible-effect retention survives worker loss, missing/malformed output and
late returns without authorizing retry. These are sound design requirements,
not evidence that the separate verifier correction or future supervised action
implementation already meets them.

No new Critical, High or Medium accessibility/interoperability design finding
was identified in the exact final brief. Its shared S2 graph limits, single
mutation/no implicit queue, 10-second cooperative interval and unchanged hard
worker deadline remain bounded requirements, not hard per-getter guarantees.
The later frozen action matrix must still establish executable profiles, every
exact effect/unrelated-field assertion, zero/one-call accounting, original-byte
evidence admission and authentic first/repeat reviews; this design approval
does not replace any of those gates.
